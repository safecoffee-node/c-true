On the heels of the rainbow the people in the west know the rain is too late and too brief to be of any real significance; it is too late.

They run in delirious circles nevertheless, to collect what they can in buckets, in old milk tins, anything that will hold water. The wells will run again, they will survive this year. They say a prayer perhaps for those who did not last long enough to be able to say this.

In the South the fields of war are silenced by rain. The drops clatter down like the rattle of bones in a grey mist of moving ghosts, like the echo of laughter, like the shadow of weeping.

In the offices of the government the clerks and the murrasalas are thankful because once more the hand of God has intervened and saved them the trouble of doing anything. They sit down and order more tea.

Rainmaker, 1989
